What worked, and what still needs improvement
Strict response validation contained malformed output. Contracts distinguished
passing/current evidence from stale, blocked or incomplete work. The agents kept
strict reference fixtures and reported the seeded failures rather than weakening
them. Delivered guidance was followed by a bounded12-capture probe, correct raw
base64 replay and a report check that no longer modified tracked source. These
are observed sequences, not causal estimates.

The largest efficiency failures were runtime and context constraints:
1. Rights were split among agents, while returning child allowance required the
   parent to afford a work call first. Coordination calls continued after useful
   work was unaffordable; renewal proposals repeatedly hit cooldown/minimum rules.
2. Large tool output was truncated and findings were not consistently retained
   in replacement memory. Agents repeatedly described already repaired stats/help
   behavior as unresolved because they lacked current complete evidence.
3. Global source freshness invalidated scoped receipts after report/coverage edits,
   producing repeated checks and preventing current handoff acceptance.
4. Moving reports to /tmp fixed tracked-source writes, but every execution creates
   a fresh sandbox. Agents regenerated whole suites to recover vanished reports.
   V008 clarified same-execution consumption, but arrived too late for delivery.

Future implementation priorities: affordable control-phase funding decisions;
clear proposal withdrawal/cooldown semantics; durable compact tool summaries;
explicit temporary-file lifetime; coordinated final source freeze; and separate
incomplete-checkpoint selection from verified completion. These follow-up changes
were not hot-loaded or used to rerun this experiment. Existing repository fixes
for hiring/memory instructions, large-command receipts, required private action
errors and probe bounds remain separate from the frozen v18 treatment.
